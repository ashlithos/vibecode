Five principles

What good taste in growth UX comes from

Why people act

Motivation × Ability × Prompt

BJ Fogg: a behavior happens only when all three meet. Most nudges fail because they only add a prompt. Make the action easier first.

When to show it

Trigger on behavior, not on login

Show a nudge right after something the user did, such as a third manual export or a first successful run. Never just because the page loaded.

What to show first

Value before asks

Show results on sample data before asking for a connection. Let them share a result before asking them to invite teammates.

Who's the hero

Make the user feel capable

Kathy Sierra: people don't love a product for being impressive. They love it because it makes them better at their job. Nudges should teach, not advertise.

How you'll know

A metric and a guardrail

Every pattern ships with a number it should move and a number it must not hurt, such as dismiss rate or task completion.

Part 3 · Team guidance

House rules and a weekly ritual

Attention budget

  • 01One push per session. At most one level-5-or-higher surface per session, across every team. Growth, product and marketing share this budget.
  • 02Never interrupt a running task. Nothing above level 4 appears while a query or agent run is in progress or the user is typing.
  • 03Dismissed means no. After a dismissal, the same nudge stays away for at least 14 days. Two dismissals retire it for that user.
  • 04A quieter fallback for every push. If a modal is dismissed, the message lives on as a badge or a changelog entry.
  • 05Respect the expert. Power users get fewer nudges. Hide coaching once someone has used the feature 3 times.
  • 06Ship with a kill switch. Every nudge sits behind a flag and has a named owner and an end date.

Growth crit: 30 minutes, weekly

  • 5 minOne person brings two screenshots from another product: one good nudge and one bad one.
  • 10 minRun each through the Surface Picker. Was its loudness within the ceiling? Was the trigger based on behavior?
  • 10 minApply the lesson to one of our flows. Sketch the quieter version.
  • 5 minAdd the example to the atlas, or change a rule if we've learned something new.
